The Big Idea

What is an accumulation function?

Imagine you're tracking how much water fills a tank. The rate of flow changes over time — sometimes it flows fast, sometimes slow. An accumulation function $F(x)$ tells you the total amount collected from a starting time $a$ up to time $x$.

Mathematically, we write: $F(x) = \displaystyle\int_a^x f(t)\,dt$, where $f(t)$ is the rate function.

The Fundamental Theorem of Calculus — Part 1
$$\frac{d}{dx}\left[\int_a^x f(t)\,dt\right] = f(x)$$

If $f$ is continuous on $[a,b]$ and $F(x) = \int_a^x f(t)\,dt$, then $F'(x) = f(x)$.

In plain English…

The derivative of an area-accumulation function undoes the integral. If you integrate a function $f$ from a fixed point $a$ to a variable point $x$, and then differentiate the result with respect to $x$, you simply get $f(x)$ back.

Why does this make sense intuitively?

Think about the area under $f$ from $a$ to $x$. When $x$ increases by a tiny amount $\Delta x$, the new area added is approximately $f(x) \cdot \Delta x$ — a thin rectangle with height $f(x)$ and width $\Delta x$. So the rate of change of the area is $\frac{\Delta A}{\Delta x} \approx f(x)$. As $\Delta x \to 0$, this becomes exactly $f(x)$.

The Chain Rule Extension

When the upper limit is a function of $x$ (e.g. $g(x)$), apply the chain rule:

$$\frac{d}{dx}\left[\int_a^{g(x)} f(t)\,dt\right] = f(g(x)) \cdot g'(x)$$

Important edge case: If both limits are functions of $x$, use the property $\int_a^b = \int_a^c + \int_c^b$ to split it.

What happens when limits are swapped?

If the variable is in the lower limit: $\dfrac{d}{dx}\displaystyle\int_x^a f(t)\,dt = -f(x)$. Flipping the limits negates the integral.

Worked Examples

Problem

Let $F(x) = \displaystyle\int_1^x (3t^2 + 2)\,dt$. Find $F'(x)$.

1

Identify the setup. $f(t) = 3t^2 + 2$ is the integrand. The upper limit is simply $x$, and the lower limit is the constant $a = 1$. FTC Part 1 applies directly.

2

Check continuity. $f(t) = 3t^2 + 2$ is a polynomial — continuous everywhere. The FTC applies on any interval.

3

Apply FTC Part 1. Differentiate by plugging $x$ in for $t$:

$$F'(x) = f(x) = 3x^2 + 2$$
Answer: $F'(x) = 3x^2 + 2$
Notice: we never actually evaluated the integral! FTC Part 1 lets us skip that entirely.

Problem

Let $G(x) = \displaystyle\int_0^{x^3} \sin(t)\,dt$. Find $G'(x)$.

1

Identify the setup. The upper limit is $g(x) = x^3$, not just $x$. We need the chain rule extension of FTC Part 1.

2

Find $g'(x)$. Differentiate the upper limit: $g(x) = x^3$, so $g'(x) = 3x^2$.

3

Apply the chain rule formula.

$$G'(x) = f(g(x)) \cdot g'(x) = \sin(x^3) \cdot 3x^2$$
Answer: $G'(x) = 3x^2\sin(x^3)$
Key: substitute $g(x)$ into $f(t)$, then multiply by $g'(x)$.

Problem

Let $H(x) = \displaystyle\int_x^5 e^{t^2}\,dt$. Find $H'(x)$.

1

The variable is in the lower limit. This requires flipping. Use the identity: $\displaystyle\int_x^5 f(t)\,dt = -\int_5^x f(t)\,dt$.

2

Rewrite: $H(x) = -\displaystyle\int_5^x e^{t^2}\,dt$

3

Apply FTC Part 1 with the negative sign:

$$H'(x) = -e^{x^2}$$
Answer: $H'(x) = -e^{x^2}$
Important: Swapping limits introduces a negative sign. Don't forget it!

Real-World Applications

FTC Part 1 isn't just an abstract rule — it describes how accumulation and rate of change interact everywhere in science and daily life.

🌊

Water Tank Volume

If $f(t)$ is the flow rate (liters/min), then $F(x) = \int_0^x f(t)\,dt$ is the total volume at time $x$. FTC says $F'(x) = f(x)$: the rate of fill equals the current flow rate. ✓

🚗

Position & Velocity

If $v(t)$ is a car's velocity, then $x(t) = \int_0^t v(s)\,ds$ gives its position. FTC Part 1 confirms $x'(t) = v(t)$ — position's derivative is velocity.

💊

Drug Concentration

$C(x) = \int_0^x r(t)\,dt$ tracks total drug absorbed. FTC tells doctors that $C'(x) = r(x)$, the absorption rate at time $x$, letting them adjust dosages dynamically.

☀️

Solar Energy Collected

$E(x) = \int_6^x P(t)\,dt$ gives total energy (kWh) since 6 AM. FTC says $E'(x) = P(x)$: the power output at this moment equals the rate of energy collection right now.
