The Big Idea

What is an accumulation function?

Imagine tracking how much water fills a tank. The rate of flow changes over time. An accumulation function \(F(x)\) tells you the total amount collected from a starting time \(a\) up to time \(x\).

Mathematically: \(F(x)=\displaystyle\int_a^x f(t)\,dt\), where \(f(t)\) is the rate function.

Fundamental Theorem of Calculus — Part 1
\[\frac{d}{dx}\!\left[\int_a^x f(t)\,dt\right]=f(x)\]

If \(f\) is continuous on \([a,b]\) and \(F(x)=\int_a^x f(t)\,dt\), then \(F'(x)=f(x)\).

In plain English…

Integrating \(f\) from a fixed point \(a\) to a variable upper limit \(x\), then differentiating with respect to \(x\), simply gives you \(f(x)\) back. Integration and differentiation undo each other.

Why does this make sense intuitively?

When \(x\) increases by a tiny \(\Delta x\), the new sliver of area added is roughly \(f(x)\cdot\Delta x\). So \(\tfrac{\Delta A}{\Delta x}\approx f(x)\), and as \(\Delta x\to 0\) this becomes exactly \(f(x)\).

The Chain Rule Extension

When the upper limit is a function \(g(x)\):

\[\frac{d}{dx}\!\left[\int_a^{g(x)}f(t)\,dt\right]=f(g(x))\cdot g'(x)\]

Both limits variable? Split with a constant \(c\): \(\int_{h(x)}^{g(x)}=\int_c^{g(x)}-\int_c^{h(x)}\), then apply the chain rule to each piece.

Variable in the lower limit?

Flip limits and negate: \(\dfrac{d}{dx}\displaystyle\int_x^a f(t)\,dt=-f(x)\).

Worked Examples

Problem

Let \(F(x)=\displaystyle\int_1^x(3t^2+2)\,dt\). Find \(F'(x)\).

1

Identify the setup. \(f(t)=3t^2+2\). Upper limit is just \(x\); lower limit is the constant \(a=1\). FTC Part 1 applies directly.

2

Check continuity. \(f(t)=3t^2+2\) is a polynomial — continuous everywhere. FTC applies on any interval.

3

Apply FTC Part 1. Plug \(x\) in for \(t\):

\[F'(x)=f(x)=3x^2+2\]
Answer: \(F'(x)=3x^2+2\)
We never evaluated the integral! FTC Part 1 lets us skip that entirely.

Problem

Let \(G(x)=\displaystyle\int_0^{x^3}\sin(t)\,dt\). Find \(G'(x)\).

1

Identify the setup. The upper limit is \(g(x)=x^3\), not just \(x\). We need the chain rule extension.

2

Find \(g'(x)\). \(g(x)=x^3\Rightarrow g'(x)=3x^2\).

3

Apply the chain rule formula:

\[G'(x)=f(g(x))\cdot g'(x)=\sin(x^3)\cdot 3x^2\]
Answer: \(G'(x)=3x^2\sin(x^3)\)
Substitute \(g(x)\) into \(f(t)\), then multiply by \(g'(x)\).

Problem

Let \(H(x)=\displaystyle\int_x^5 e^{t^2}\,dt\). Find \(H'(x)\).

1

Variable is in the lower limit. Use: \(\displaystyle\int_x^5 f(t)\,dt=-\int_5^x f(t)\,dt\).

2

Rewrite: \(H(x)=-\displaystyle\int_5^x e^{t^2}\,dt\).

3

Apply FTC Part 1 with the negative sign:

\[H'(x)=-e^{x^2}\]
Answer: \(H'(x)=-e^{x^2}\)
Swapping limits introduces a negative sign. Don’t forget it!

Real-World Applications

FTC Part 1 describes how accumulation and rate of change interact everywhere in science and daily life.

🌊

Water Tank Volume

If \(f(t)\) is the flow rate (L/min), then \(F(x)=\int_0^x f(t)\,dt\) is total volume at time \(x\). FTC says \(F'(x)=f(x)\): rate of fill equals current flow rate.

🚗

Position & Velocity

If \(v(t)\) is a car’s velocity, then \(x(t)=\int_0^t v(s)\,ds\) gives position. FTC confirms \(x'(t)=v(t)\) — position’s derivative is velocity.

💊

Drug Concentration

\(C(x)=\int_0^x r(t)\,dt\) tracks total drug absorbed. FTC tells doctors \(C'(x)=r(x)\), the current absorption rate, helping adjust dosages.

☀

Solar Energy

\(E(x)=\int_6^x P(t)\,dt\) gives total kWh since 6 AM. FTC says \(E'(x)=P(x)\): the power output right now equals the current rate of energy collection.
